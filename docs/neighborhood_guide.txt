Westbrook University: Off-Campus Neighborhood Guide
Source: Westbrook Student Government Housing Committee

---

UNIVERSITY DISTRICT (0-10 min walk to campus)

The University District covers the blocks immediately surrounding Westbrook University's main quad. This is the most convenient location for students but also the most expensive.

Pros: Walking distance to all academic buildings, library, and student union. Active street life with cafes, restaurants, and convenience stores on every block. Well-lit, heavy foot traffic until late at night. Campus security patrols extend into this area.

Cons: Highest rent prices in the Westbrook student area. Noise from bars on University Ave especially Thursday-Saturday nights. Very limited parking, street parking is metered and competitive. Buildings are older on average.

Notable apartments: Several small landlords operate converted houses and older apartment buildings. Maplewood Apartments is on the edge of this zone (10 min walk to main quad).

Grocery options: Small market on University Ave (expensive, limited selection). Trader Joe's is a 15-min walk east.

---

CEDAR STREET CORRIDOR (10-15 min walk to campus)

The Cedar Street area has been developed more recently and has a mix of newer apartment buildings, restaurants, and bars. It's become popular with graduate students and young professionals as well as upperclassmen.

Pros: Newer buildings with more amenities. Route 3 bus is very frequent (every 10 min daytime). Good restaurant and bar scene. Some newer buildings have gyms, rooftops, and in-unit laundry.

Cons: Bar district means significant noise Thursday-Saturday after 10pm. Slightly more expensive than comparable units farther from campus. Parking is difficult without a garage.

Notable apartments: The Lofts on Cedar is the main high-end option here.

---

NORTH CAMPUS RESIDENTIAL (12-18 min walk to campus)

A quieter, more residential area north of campus. Mostly 2-3 story apartment buildings and converted houses. Mix of students and longer-term residents.

Pros: Quieter than University District or Cedar Street. More affordable. Larger units for the price. Pinecrest Apartments is in this area. Route 12 bus runs along the main road.

Cons: Walking to campus takes 12-18 min. Less active street life — fewer restaurants and shops within walking distance. Some blocks have limited street lighting.

Notable apartments: Pinecrest Student Apartments (12 min walk to campus).

Grocery options: Small Aldi one mile north (20 min walk or 5 min drive). Farmers market on Saturday mornings at North Campus Park (seasonal).

---

RIVERSIDE AREA (20+ min walk, served by bus)

The Riverside area is about 20 minutes on foot from campus but well-served by the Route 7 bus. It's primarily residential with very little commercial development, meaning a quieter environment but more car-dependent lifestyle.

Pros: Significantly cheaper rent. More spacious apartments. Quiet neighborhood. Riverside Commons has a pool. Free parking. Mix of students and non-student residents creates a calmer atmosphere.

Cons: Bus route 7 stops at midnight, no late night service. Must plan around bus schedule or have a car. Fewer walkable amenities. Further from campus social life.

Notable apartments: Riverside Commons (20 min walk or 10 min on Route 7 bus).

Grocery options: Shaw's Supermarket 0.5 miles from Riverside Commons (10 min walk, well-stocked, full selection).

---

COMPARING YOUR OPTIONS: A QUICK GUIDE

If you want walkability and don't mind paying for it: University District or Cedar Street
If you want the best value and have a car or can work the bus schedule: Riverside Area
If you want a balance of price and walkability with a student community feel: North Campus Residential
If you want luxury amenities and can afford $1,400+/month: The Lofts on Cedar
If you want the cheapest option near campus: Pinecrest (2BR split) or Riverside Commons 1BR
