FINDING A ROOMMATE NEAR WESTBROOK

The best places to find roommates for off-campus housing at Westbrook:
1. The Westbrook University Housing Board on Facebook (over 4,000 members) — post what you're looking for and your budget
2. The r/WestbrookU subreddit "Housing Megathread" pinned at the start of each semester
3. Westbrook University's official Roommate Matching Program (sign up through offcampus@westbrook.edu) — underused but good for finding compatible matches with a personality questionnaire
4. The Housing Board in the Student Union (physical corkboard, surprisingly still active)
5. Through your major's Discord or GroupMe — useful for finding roommates in the same program who have similar schedules

THE ROOMMATE AGREEMENT: WHAT TO COVER

A written roommate agreement (separate from your lease) prevents most roommate conflicts. Cover these before you move in:

Finances: How will rent be split? How will utilities be split? What's the process if one person can't pay on time? Who holds the utility accounts? Pro tip: use Splitwise to track shared expenses.

Guests and overnight visitors: How often is okay? Any quiet hours? Is a partner essentially moving in a third resident? Agree on this before it becomes an issue.

Cleaning: Who cleans what, how often? A specific schedule is better than "we'll figure it out." Common conflict areas: dishes, bathroom cleaning, taking out trash.

Noise and study time: Quiet hours expectation? Can one person play music or have TV on while the other studies? What's the protocol?

Temperature: This sounds trivial but is a real source of conflict. Agree on a thermostat range (e.g., 68-72°F) before winter.

Shared groceries and food: What's shared, what's off-limits? Label your food or have a clear conversation upfront.

COMMON ROOMMATE CONFLICT PATTERNS AT WESTBROOK

(Compiled from the Off-Campus Student Services office)

#1 most common issue: One roommate invites a partner to stay over regularly without prior discussion. Address the guest policy before this happens.

#2: Cleaning standards mismatch. What one person considers "clean enough" another considers a problem. Be specific in your agreement.

#3: Noise during finals. Even compatible roommates can conflict when one has exams at different times. Agree in advance that during exam weeks, quiet hours expand.

#4: Splitting costs for shared items (dish soap, toilet paper, coffee maker). Decide upfront who buys what or whether you put $20 each into a shared fund per month.

#5: One roommate's lease situation changes (study abroad, co-op away from Westbrook). Know your lease's subletting policy (most near Westbrook prohibit it) and have a plan.

SHARED LEASE RESPONSIBILITIES

When you're both on a lease, you're both fully responsible for the entire rent — not just your share. This is called joint and several liability. What this means practically: if your roommate doesn't pay their share, the landlord can come after you for the full amount. Before signing a joint lease with someone, make sure you trust them financially or have a plan for this scenario.

If your roommate moves out before the lease ends, the remaining tenant is typically still responsible for the full rent. Know this risk going in.

Best practice: have a written plan for what happens if one of you needs to leave early. Options include finding a subletter (if lease allows), negotiating with the landlord directly, or one person buying out the other.
