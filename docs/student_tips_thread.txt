Posted by wbu_2024_grad:
What I wish I knew: heat included vs. not included is a HUGE difference. I moved from a heat-included apartment (Maplewood, $850/month) to a cheaper place ($720/month) thinking I was saving money. My heating bill in January was $180. I ended up spending MORE. Always calculate total cost including utilities before comparing rent prices.

Posted by westbrook_housing_veteran:
Three years off-campus at Westbrook, here's what I know:
1. Sign early. I mean October/November early for August move-in. I missed out on a great place because I waited until February.
2. Always negotiate. Most landlords near Westbrook won't budge on rent but will on other things: parking included, lease start date flexibility, first month discounted, new appliances before you move in. Ask.
3. The Westbrook Off-Campus Student Services office (Room 201, Student Services Building) has a list of landlords who've had complaints filed against them. You can ask to see it. It's not public but they'll tell you if a specific landlord has a pattern of complaints.

Posted by wbu_engineering_student:
Hot take: the distance from your apartment to YOUR specific buildings matters more than distance to "campus." I lived at Maplewood thinking it was a 10 min walk to campus. It was 10 min to the main quad, but 20 min to the engineering building on the east side. Map your actual route to your department, not to the front gates.

Posted by anonymous_wbu_2023:
Bedbugs. I know no one wants to talk about this but it happened to me in a Westbrook off-campus apartment (not one of the big complexes, a private landlord). Signs to check before you move in: look at mattress seams if the place is furnished, check along baseboards, look for small rust-colored stains on walls near the bed. If you get them, your landlord is required to pay for extermination under Westbrook housing code. Document everything and report immediately in writing.

Posted by wbu_budget_tips:
Actual monthly budget breakdown I used living off-campus at Westbrook:
- Rent (1BR at Pinecrest): $720
- Utilities (electric + internet, heat included): $90
- Groceries (Aldi + occasional Trader Joe's): $200-250
- Transportation (bus is free with student ID): $0
- Laundry: $20 (coin laundry in building)
Total: approx. $1,050-1,100/month for everything
Compare to campus housing which runs $1,300-1,500/month including a meal plan you won't fully use.

Posted by wbu_international_housing_tips:
For international students specifically:
- Some landlords near Westbrook require a US-based co-signer if you don't have US credit history. Ask about this BEFORE applying — it avoids a lot of wasted time.
- Alternatively, Riverside Commons and Pinecrest have historically accepted larger security deposits (2-3 months) in lieu of a co-signer.
- Your F-1 status doesn't affect your tenant rights — you have the same rights as any other tenant under Westbrook city law.
- The international student office can provide a letter verifying your enrollment status, which many landlords accept as part of the application.

Posted by wbu_senior_advice:
The real unwritten rule of Westbrook off-campus housing: the longer you stay, the better deal you get. Landlords HATE turnover — vacant units cost them money. If you're a reliable tenant who pays on time and doesn't cause problems, you have more leverage at renewal than you think. I've had my rent frozen for two years by simply asking nicely and pointing out my track record. The worst they can say is no.

Posted by wbu_grad_student_perspective:
As a grad student comparing off-campus options: The Lofts is genuinely the best if budget allows — the in-unit laundry alone is worth a lot of time. For those of us with stipends, Riverside Commons is the realistic choice. The bus situation is manageable if you build your schedule around it. One thing I didn't expect: Riverside is actually MORE quiet for working from home than any on-campus housing. The mix of non-student residents really does change the atmosphere.
